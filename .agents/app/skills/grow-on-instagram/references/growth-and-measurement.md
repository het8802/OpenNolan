# Growth, Trust, and Measurement

Use this reference for account strategy, a 30-day plan, conversion design, or performance review.

## Establish the initial growth system

Begin with one narrow topic space and a clear audience. Use the first body of posts to learn, not to prove a fixed formula.

1. Publish at a cadence the team can sustain with quality.
2. Collect enough comparable posts to see recurring topic, hook, format, and proof patterns.
3. Identify the topics that repeatedly earn both attention and the right downstream signal.
4. Promote those topics into pillars.
5. Improve one controllable element per subsequent post.

Early volume is useful because it creates evidence. It does not make weak or unfocused content effective by itself.

## Plan a 30-post test matrix

Use a practical 5 × 3 × 2 planning grid:

- Pick **five** audience-relevant subtopics where the creator has a credible perspective.
- Pair each with **three** formats: two dependable formats and one higher-effort test.
- Produce each combination in **two** visual-layout treatments.

This produces 30 planned prompts. It does not require publishing every day; sequence the plan around capacity and feedback. Record the variables so you can later distinguish a winning topic from a winning treatment.

## Build trust with proof

Use the strongest honest evidence available. A useful hierarchy, from weaker to stronger in many contexts, is:

1. Third-party facts or studies
2. A generic testimonial
3. Borrowed authority or a cited expert framework
4. The creator’s own documented result
5. Multiple relevant client/customer results
6. Live demonstration of ability
7. An authentic audience signal
8. A relevant endorsement from someone the target viewer already trusts

Context changes the order. The rule is simple: show what a skeptical viewer needs to believe the claim. Disclose limits, do not fabricate proof, and avoid treating follower count as a universal authority signal.

## Design for a viewer’s next step

A feed post may be the only contact a viewer has with the account. Give it a complete payoff, then choose one route:

- **Binge route:** a named series, related post, playlist, or profile promise.
- **Lead route:** a genuinely useful free resource, email list, or low-friction opt-in.
- **Depth route:** a long-form article/video, community, product, or service appropriate to the promise.

Use a single CTA that matches the post. Avoid irrelevant comment bait or hiding the answer behind an action.

## Create a coherent brand world

Use these elements only when they make the experience more recognizable and navigable:

- **Surfaces:** profile, posts, email, long-form, landing pages, community, and product.
- **Objects:** a recurring prop, artifact, tool, or product that has meaning in the work.
- **Characters:** recurring collaborators, customers, or roles when they add story value.
- **Citizens:** viewers, users, and buyers who can become participants and advocates.
- **Aesthetics:** repeatable color, type, textures, sound, and visual grammar.
- **Interoperability:** intentional bridges that move a viewer between surfaces.

Consistency should make navigation easier, not turn every post into a brand exercise.

## Make content shareable without gaming it

Design something a specific person would want to send: a useful explanation, a rare proof artifact, a generous mention, or a visually distinctive execution. When making work intended for an influential peer, make it genuinely useful to that person’s audience; do not flatter, tag-spam, or pressure them.

## Review the data each week

Capture at minimum:

| Input | Attention | Depth | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| topic, angle, format, hook, visual, proof, CTA | impressions, views, hold rate, retention | shares, saves, comments, profile visits, follows | leads, qualified conversations, purchases, revenue quality |

Compare posts of similar age and distribution. Look for repeated combinations rather than one outlier. Keep a hypothesis log: “For [audience], [mechanism] may improve [signal] because [reason].” Design the next post to test that hypothesis.
