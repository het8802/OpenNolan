# Storytelling and Retention

Use this reference for narrative Reels, educational posts that need more tension, or retention audits.

## Pass the three-part story test

Before scripting, make the viewer able to answer yes to all three questions:

1. **Relevant:** Is this connected to a problem, identity, job, interest, or desired result I care about?
2. **Novel:** Is there a genuine new fact, frame, comparison, or observation?
3. **Interesting:** Is there enough stakes, consequence, uncertainty, pain, or outcome to make the answer matter?

Increase relevance by naming the audience or pain. Increase novelty by finding a real change or reframing a familiar fact. Increase interestingness by making the cost, outcome, or character stakes concrete.

## Make a familiar topic feel newly relevant

Use the novelty sequence when the underlying topic is familiar:

1. **New reveal:** state what changed or what the audience did not know.
2. **Contrast frame:** show the old assumption beside the more useful interpretation.
3. **Timeliness:** add a real reason this matters now, if one exists.
4. **Specific proof:** offer the evidence, example, demonstration, or source that justifies the reveal.

Do not use novelty language without proof. An exaggerated reveal that cannot survive the payoff creates distrust.

## Load anticipation without withholding the value

Open a question the intended viewer wants answered. Delay the final answer only while adding value that raises the stakes or increases confidence:

- Reveal an example that complicates the first assumption.
- Show a proof artifact, failed attempt, or smaller discovery.
- Add a subplot that affects the decision.
- Define the cost of choosing the old approach.

After a payoff, open one new relevant question if the post has another useful section. Do not keep loops open merely to prolong the video; close them with a clear answer.

## Re-hook the middle

Longer Reels need a deliberate transition from the first valuable segment to the next. Use a re-hook after the first complete idea or around the point where the story changes—not a rigid timestamp.

Useful re-hook moves:

- “But the more important part is…”
- “That explains what; here is how…”
- “The result looked good until…”
- A new visual, comparison, or on-screen question.
- A fresh proof item that changes the viewer’s prediction.

The re-hook must create a new reason to continue, not repeat the opening claim.

## Use a villain to sharpen an educational story

Define the desired approach as the hero. Define the antagonist as one of:

- the old way of doing the work;
- a false or outdated belief; or
- the shared frustration the old belief produces.

Place the hero and antagonist in contrast throughout the explanation. Keep the villain proportional and real; do not vilify people or invent a problem to create drama.

## Select the narrative container

Choose a story type before writing the script, then choose a structure and visual treatment that suits it:

| Story type | Core question | Strong proof | Suitable visual treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origin/change | “What changed?” | timeline, before/after, artifact | direct-to-camera plus evidence |
| Failure/lesson | “Why did this fail?” | decision trail, error, recovery | screen, teardown, reenactment |
| Case study | “How did this result happen?” | steps, constraints, outcome | graphics plus proof artifacts |
| Myth reversal | “What is the better model?” | comparison, counterexample | contrast edit or whiteboard |
| Investigation | “What is really causing this?” | observations, tests, source material | evidence-led sequence |
| Demonstration | “Can it be done?” | live process | screen capture, hands, object |

## Script audit

- Does the opening create relevance, novelty, and interest?
- Is there one central question rather than several competing questions?
- Does each beat increase understanding, stakes, or evidence?
- Does the middle earn a re-hook with a genuine new segment?
- Is the payoff direct, specific, and proportionate to the promise?
- Does the CTA follow naturally from the completed value?
