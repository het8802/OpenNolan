See it in action

A product demo video has one job: make a stranger understand, in seconds, why your thing is worth their time. That's harder than it sounds, because the natural instinct is to start at your login screen and walk through every menu. By the time you reach the part that matters, the viewer is already gone. The demo that converts does the opposite — it opens on the result and works backward.

The villain here is the same one every builder fights: the editing tax. To make a demo that holds attention, you're told you need to record clean screen captures, cut out the dead air, drop in callouts and zooms, add captions, score it with music, and trim it to length. That's a full afternoon of editing for thirty seconds of video, every time your product changes — which, if you're shipping, is constantly.

We built OpenNolan because we're builders too, and we'd rather demo the product than edit the demo. It's a Mac app where an AI agent turns your raw screen recordings into a finished vertical cut — outcome-first, captioned, paced for the feed — then hands you a real timeline so the final version says exactly what you mean. Below is the actual playbook, useful whether or not you ever open the app.

Lead with the outcome, not the UI tour

Show what it does before you show how it works.

The single biggest mistake in product demos is starting with the interface. Nobody cares about your sidebar until they care about what your sidebar does for them. So open on the after: the finished result, the saved hour, the problem visibly solved. Show the destination first, then let the steps earn their place by explaining how you got there.

Practically, that means your first frame should be the payoff — the dashboard already populated, the report already generated, the thing that makes someone go "wait, how." Curiosity is what buys you the next ten seconds. A UI tour answers questions nobody asked yet; an outcome creates the question that makes them watch.

  • First frame is the result, not the home screen
  • Tie every click to a benefit, not a feature name
  • Cut the setup, the login, and the empty states

Screen recording, screenshot proof, and explainer animations

Three kinds of footage carry a demo. Screen recording is the spine — capture the real flow at full resolution, then trim hard so every second moves. Screenshot proof is your evidence: a results graph, a before/after, a real number on screen makes the claim land in a way narration never can. And explainer animations fill the gaps a raw recording can't show — the concept behind the feature, the data flowing, the thing that happens off-screen.

You don't need all three in every demo, but you almost always want recording plus one form of proof. The recording shows it's real; the proof shows it works. Animations are the polish that makes a thirty-second clip feel like it came from a team, not a tab you forgot to close.

  • Screen recording trimmed to only the moments that move
  • Screenshot proof with the key number or result highlighted
  • Explainer animation for what the recording can't capture

Keep it short, hook in the first second, caption everything

Short-form demos live or die on three rules. Keep it short — under thirty seconds for the feed, and ruthless even then; a demo that needs two minutes needs a tighter idea. Win the first second with a real hook: open on the payoff or a pattern interrupt, never on "hey guys, today I want to show you." And caption everything, because most of the feed watches on mute — burned-in captions are what carry your value prop when the sound is off.

Then close with one clear call to action. Not three. Tell the viewer the single next step — try it, star the repo, join the list — and make it the last thing they see. A demo without a CTA is a trailer with no showtime.

  • Under 30 seconds, cut every 1 to 3 seconds
  • Hook lands the value in the first second
  • Burned-in captions for the 85% watching on mute
  • One CTA, stated plainly, at the end

The product demo video playbook, step by step

  1. Find your one outcome

    Before you record anything, name the single result that makes your product worth using — the saved hour, the finished report, the problem gone. That outcome is your opening frame and the spine of the whole demo. If you can't name one, the demo isn't ready; the idea is. In OpenNolan, you can just describe this outcome and the agent builds the cut around it.

  2. Record the real flow, then cut it hard

    Capture the actual screen flow that produces the outcome, at full resolution, in one clean take if you can. Then be ruthless: trim the login, the loading, the empty states, and every pause. Keep only the seconds that move the story. OpenNolan ingests your raw recordings and trims the dead air for you, so you record loose and let the agent tighten it.

  3. Stack proof and explainer beats

    Drop in a screenshot of the real result with the key number highlighted, and add a short animation for anything the recording can't show — the concept, the data, the off-screen magic. Proof makes the claim credible; animation makes it clear. OpenNolan adds explainer animations and auto-highlights your screenshot proof as part of the build.

  4. Open with a hook and add captions

    Lead with the payoff in the first second, never with a slow intro. Then caption everything, because most viewers watch on mute and the captions carry your message. Ride a trending sound if it fits. OpenNolan writes and burns in captions, scores the cut with music and sound effects, and paces it for vertical automatically.

  5. End on one clear CTA

    Close with a single next step — try it, star the repo, join the list — and make it the final frame the viewer sees. One ask, stated plainly. Multiple CTAs split attention and convert worse than one. In OpenNolan you can edit the closing card on the timeline so the ask matches whatever campaign the demo is for.

  6. Review on a real timeline, then ship

    Watch it once end to end and fix what's off — a caption typo, a beat that drags, a clip in the wrong order. Then post it while the feature is still fresh. OpenNolan gives you a real NLE timeline for this final pass, and because it renders once and edits stay cheap, most tweaks preview instantly so reviewing every demo is fast enough that you'll actually do it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a product demo video that converts?

Lead with the outcome, not a UI tour. Open on the result your product delivers, hook the viewer in the first second, then show the real flow trimmed tight. Add screenshot proof, burned-in captions, and one clear CTA at the end. Keep it under thirty seconds for short-form feeds where most demos are now discovered.

How long should a short-form product demo be?

For Reels, TikTok, and Shorts, aim for under thirty seconds, and cut every one to three seconds to keep attention moving. If your demo genuinely needs more, the idea is too broad — split it into several focused demos. Shorter, tighter clips travel further and earn the click better than one long walkthrough nobody finishes.

What should the first frame of a demo video be?

The payoff. Show the finished result, the solved problem, or the surprising number before you show any menus or setup. That outcome creates the curiosity that buys you the next ten seconds. Starting on your login screen or home dashboard answers questions nobody has asked yet, and that's where most demo viewers drop off.
