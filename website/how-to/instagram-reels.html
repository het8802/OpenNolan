Get the Reel specs right: 9:16, length, and safe zones

Start with the frame, because Instagram is unforgiving about it. Reels are vertical: shoot and export at 9:16, 1080×1920. Anything else gets letterboxed or cropped, and a black bar in the feed reads as low effort before a word is spoken.

Then mind the safe zones. Instagram lays its own UI over your video — the caption, username, audio tag, and the right-hand action rail of like, comment, share, and save buttons. Keep your hook text and key visuals in the middle of the frame, roughly the central 80%, so nothing important hides behind a button. On length, shorter usually wins: 7 to 20 seconds is the sweet spot for reach, because completion rate matters more than runtime, and a tight Reel is easy to rewatch.

  • Export 9:16 at 1080×1920 — full-bleed, no bars
  • Keep text and faces inside the central safe zone
  • Aim for 7–20s; reward completion, not runtime
  • Leave the bottom-left clear of Instagram's caption overlay

The hook, trending audio, and fast cuts that earn the watch

Win the first second, ride the sound, cut before they get bored.

Reach is won in the first second. Open on the payoff or a pattern interrupt — the result, the bold claim, the surprising before — not a slow intro. If your first frame is a logo or a throat-clear, the scroll wins. Want to go deeper on this one move? The anatomy of a hook breaks down why the first second decides everything.

Audio is Instagram's quiet ranking lever. Riding a trending sound from the Reels audio library gives the algorithm a reason to push your clip onto more For You pages, and the beat gives you a natural cut rhythm. Then keep it moving: a hard cut every 1 to 3 seconds resets attention and stops the brain from drifting. Flat, single-take talking is a desktop habit; the Reels feed rewards motion.

Captions, covers, and the posting playbook

Most of the feed watches on mute, so burned-in captions aren't optional — they carry the message when there's no sound, and they keep viewers reading instead of swiping. Add receipts where you can: a screenshot, a metric, a real result makes the claim land and turns watchers into clickers.

Two finishing moves decide whether a Reel earns its place. The cover, or thumbnail, is the still that represents your Reel in the grid and the Reels tab — design it with a few clear words so a browser knows the payoff before tapping. For posting, write a caption that adds context, use a handful of specific, relevant hashtags rather than a wall of generic ones, and post when your audience is actually online. Consistency beats any single perfect Reel — the algorithm rewards people who show up.

  • Burn in captions — most viewers watch on mute
  • Show proof: screenshots, stats, real results
  • Design a cover with a clear, readable payoff
  • Use a few specific hashtags, not a generic wall
  • Post consistently — cadence compounds reach

The step-by-step playbook for making an Instagram Reel

  1. Plan one clear idea and hook

    Pick a single payoff per Reel — one result, one tip, one before-and-after. Decide the first-second hook before you record: the line or visual that makes a scroller stop. If you can't say the hook in five words, the idea is too broad for the feed. In OpenNolan you can just describe this idea and let the agent shape it into a hook.

  2. Capture vertical footage at 9:16

    Record in 9:16 / 1080×1920 from the start so nothing gets cropped later. Get a few takes and a little extra B-roll — product on screen, your hands, the result. Keep faces and any on-screen text inside the central safe zone, away from Instagram's caption and button overlays. Rough, real clips are fine; the editing is the part you're about to skip.

  3. Cut to a tight, motion-driven sequence

    Trim to the moments that move the story and hard-cut every 1 to 3 seconds to reset attention. Lay it over a trending sound so the cuts ride the beat. Aim for 7 to 20 seconds total. This is the slow part by hand — in OpenNolan the agent does the cutting, timing, music, and B-roll for you, framed for vertical.

  4. Add captions, proof, and motion overlays

    Burn in captions for the mute majority, drop in a screenshot or stat as proof, and add light motion — text reveals, highlights — so the frame never sits still. OpenNolan writes and burns in the captions, auto-highlights screenshot proof, and animates overlays as part of building the cut.

  5. Design a cover and write the post

    Make a cover still with a few clear words so the Reel earns its grid spot. Write a caption that adds context, add a few specific hashtags, and pick a posting time when your audience is online. A strong cover lifts taps from the Reels tab and your profile grid, where a lot of reach quietly comes from.

  6. Review, export, and ship — then do it again

    Watch it once on mute and once with sound. Fix the one beat that drags. Export at 1080×1920 and post. Then repeat, because cadence is what compounds on Instagram. OpenNolan's render-once model makes review fast enough that you'll actually do it before every Reel goes out, so consistency stops being the thing that breaks.
