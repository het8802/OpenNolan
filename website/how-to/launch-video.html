What makes a product launch video different

Launch day is one shot. The video is the trailer, not the demo.

A launch video is not a recurring Reel and it's not a long demo. It's a one-time, high-stakes asset that lives at the top of a Product Hunt post, an X announcement thread, and a LinkedIn update on the same day. Its job is to convert attention into a click on launch day, not to keep a content calendar fed.

That changes the brief. You lead with the single biggest claim, you show the product actually working within a few seconds, you back it with real receipts, and you put your face on it so a stranger trusts a stranger. Calm, slow, and feature-by-feature is a demo. A launch video is the trailer for the demo — fast, confident, and built to be watched on mute.

One cut per platform, not one cut for all

The same launch deserves a different edit per surface. Product Hunt and X reward a tight 20-to-40-second cut that front-loads the claim and the click. LinkedIn rewards a slightly calmer, founder-forward version that reads as a story. A vertical Reel or Short reaches a colder audience who've never heard of you, so the hook has to work even harder.

Cutting four versions by hand on launch week is exactly the tax that stops founders from posting everywhere. With OpenNolan you start from one finished cut and spin variants fast, because the heavy render is already done. Common launch video mistakes to avoid

Most launch videos fail in predictable ways: they open with a logo animation instead of the claim, they bury the product reveal past the point where people have already scrolled, they show no proof so the claim feels like marketing, and they ship with no captions into a muted feed. Any one of these quietly halves your click-through on the biggest day of the quarter.

The fix is the playbook above, applied with discipline you don't have time for on launch week. That's the whole reason OpenNolan exists — to put the proven structure into every cut by default, so your one shot isn't undone by a slow first second or a missing receipt.

  • Don't open on a logo — open on the claim or the payoff
  • Don't hide the product — show it working in the first few seconds
  • Don't claim without receipts — put real numbers on screen
  • Don't ship silent — burn in captions for muted autoplay

How to make a product launch video: 5 steps

  1. Lead with the one big claim

    Decide the single sentence that makes someone stop. Not your full value prop — the boldest true thing your product does. Put it in the first second as text and as the opening line, before any logo or intro. OpenNolan opens on the payoff for you, building the hook from your brief instead of a slow build-up that loses the scroll.

  2. Show the product actually working

    Within a few seconds, cut to the product doing the thing — a screen recording, the result, the moment of magic. Talking about it is a demo; showing it is a launch. OpenNolan places your screen captures and B-roll into a fast vertical cut, so the reveal lands early instead of after a minute of setup nobody waits for.

  3. Put real proof on screen

    A claim without receipts reads as marketing. Drop in a screenshot of the metric, the chart, the waitlist count, the before-and-after — and highlight the number that matters so it can't be missed. OpenNolan adds screenshot proof with auto-highlight, so your strongest evidence is framed and called out, not buried in a tiny corner.

  4. Add your founder face for trust

    People back people on launch day. A short, direct talking-head clip — even ten seconds of you saying why you built it — converts strangers better than any voiceover. OpenNolan handles talking-head with background removal, so your face sits cleanly over the product without you setting up a studio the night before launch.

  5. Cut it per platform, with a clear CTA

    Export a tight cut for Product Hunt and X, a calmer founder-forward version for LinkedIn, and a vertical one for Reels — each ending on one clear ask: upvote, try it, join the waitlist. OpenNolan renders once so variants are cheap, and you finish each version on a real timeline before you post.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a product launch video be?

For the launch-day asset on Product Hunt and X, aim for 20 to 40 seconds — long enough to land the claim, show the product, and add proof, short enough to watch on mute. LinkedIn can run a touch longer if it reads as a founder story. The first second still decides everything, so front-load the hook regardless of length.

How do you make a product launch video without an editor?

You start from a finished cut instead of a blank timeline. With OpenNolan you drop in raw clips and a screen recording, and the AI agent builds the whole launch video — hook, product reveal, screenshot proof, captions, music, and a founder talking-head. You review and tweak on a real timeline, but you're never editing from scratch on launch week.
