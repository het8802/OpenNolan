See it in action

You run an agency. Your clients want a steady stream of Reels, TikToks, and Shorts, and your margin lives in how many you can ship per editor per week. The math is brutal: every cut means sourcing B-roll, syncing captions, finding music, and trimming for vertical — multiplied by every client on the roster. Output is capped by headcount, and headcount eats the margin you were hired to protect.

The villain here is the editing tax at scale. One reel is a manageable afternoon. Twenty reels a week across eight clients is a treadmill that no amount of hustle outruns — and the work gets less consistent the faster you push it. Quality drifts, deadlines slip, and you end up either hiring ahead of revenue or shipping cuts you're not proud of.

OpenNolan is a Mac app where an AI agent makes the whole first cut — B-roll and images pulled from the web, music, sound effects, captions, rapid cuts, and motion, all tuned for 9:16. Your editor doesn't start from a blank timeline; they start from a finished draft and spend their time on judgment, not grunt work. We're builders too, and we'd rather your team review than rebuild.

A repeatable playbook for high-volume client work

Throughput comes from a system, not from working later. The pattern that scales for agencies is the same every time: feed in the raw clips for a client, let the agent build the first cut against the short-form playbook, then have a human review and approve before it leaves the building. No reel ships without eyes on it — but no editor burns an afternoon getting it to the review stage.

Because the draft already follows the rules that make short-form travel, your editors review against a consistent baseline instead of reinventing structure per client. That's where consistency at volume actually comes from.

  • Hook in the first second, cuts every 1–3 seconds
  • Captions burned in for sound-off viewing
  • Vertical 9:16 framing tuned for Reels, TikTok, and Shorts
  • Receipts — screenshots and proof — placed where they land

How OpenNolan lifts output without lifting headcount

Your editors stop building reels from scratch and start reviewing finished drafts — that's the throughput unlock.

The agent does the heavy, repetitive part of every cut: it places clips, pulls supporting images and B-roll from the web, adds music and sound effects, writes captions, animates overlays, and assembles a vertical edit. That's the work that used to define how many reels one editor could touch in a day. Move it off their plate and the per-editor ceiling rises without a new hire.

Then there's the part that keeps your delivery timeline honest. OpenNolan renders once and edits cheap — after the first render, most changes (reorder, retrim, retime, transitions, reposition, text) preview instantly without re-rendering. Only an AI-composition scene re-renders, and only the one you changed. Client comes back with a note the morning it's due? Your editor makes the change and sees it immediately, instead of waiting on an export queue.

  • First cut built by the agent, not by a billed hour
  • Instant preview on revisions — no re-render to see a change
  • Only the changed scene re-renders, so polish is fast
  • A real NLE timeline for the final human pass before handoff

Margin control with bring-your-own-key

Per-video cost is what makes or breaks agency economics, and most tools hide it behind a seat-based subscription that climbs as your team grows. OpenNolan is bring-your-own-key: you add your own AI provider key and pay the providers directly — no markup, no subscription, no per-seat tax. Your cost per reel is the actual compute it took, which you can see and control.

It's also a native Mac app that runs local-first, so your clients' raw footage stays on your machines. And it's open source. For an agency, that means the throughput tool sitting at the center of delivery isn't a black box you're renting by the seat.
