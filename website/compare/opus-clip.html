You're a builder, not an editor. What you actually want is a vertical video that stops the scroll and sends people to your product — without losing a week to it. The villain here is the editing tax: the unspoken rule that to get seen, you first have to become a full-time video editor chasing trending audio, cutting B-roll, and syncing captions.

Opus Clip and OpenNolan both fight that tax, but from different starting points. Opus Clip is built to repurpose — feed it a long podcast, webinar, or stream and it finds the strong moments and turns them into short clips with captions and a virality score. If you already produce long videos, that's genuinely useful.

OpenNolan starts earlier. You drop in raw clips or just describe the video, and an AI agent creates an original short — pulling B-roll and images from the web, adding music, sound effects, captions, rapid cuts and motion, all tuned for vertical. No long video required. Then you fine-tune any cut on a real timeline. We're builders too, so we built the guide we wanted: Mac-native, bring-your-own-key, open source.

Repurpose vs create: where each tool starts

Opus Clip finds the best moments in a video you already made. OpenNolan makes the reel you don't have yet.

The cleanest way to choose is to look at your input. Opus Clip needs an existing long video — it's a slicing engine. Give it an hour of footage and it surfaces the clip-worthy moments. If your bottleneck is "I have lots of long content and no time to cut it down," that's exactly its job.

OpenNolan's input is rawer: loose clips from your phone or screen recorder, a few screenshots, or nothing but an idea. There may be no long video to repurpose at all. The agent assembles an original reel from those pieces — finding supporting B-roll, writing the hook, scoring it with music, and pacing it for the feed.

  • Opus Clip: long video in, multiple clips out
  • OpenNolan: raw clips or an idea in, one finished original reel out
  • OpenNolan adds web B-roll, images, music, SFX, captions, and motion the agent sources for you

How the editing actually happens

Opus Clip is an automated web app: it processes your upload, returns clips, and you tune captions and framing in the browser. It's fast and it's good at what it does — turning long-form into shareable cuts.

OpenNolan pairs an AI agent with a real editor. The agent does the heavy lift — composition, cuts, B-roll, captions, animated overlays — then hands you a proper timeline. Thanks to a render-once, edit-cheap model, most edits (reorder, retrim, retime, reposition, text) preview instantly without re-rendering. You get automation when you want it and full manual control when you need it. If you'd rather see the playbook the agent follows, read what makes a hook work.

  • Agent builds the whole cut, you adjust on a timeline
  • Render-once, edit-cheap: most tweaks preview instantly
  • Cuts, overlays, text, images, audio, transitions, crop, and speed all editable by hand

Platform, pricing, and where your files live

Opus Clip is a web app on a subscription and credit model — your video goes up to their servers, and you pay a recurring plan. For many teams that's the right trade.

OpenNolan is a native Mac app that runs local-first: your files stay on your machine. Instead of a subscription, you bring your own AI provider key and pay providers directly — no markup, no account required. And it's open source, so you can read exactly what it does. If you're shipping product content week after week, see why this fits a SaaS workflow.

  • Mac-native and local-first vs web app
  • Bring-your-own-key, pay providers directly vs subscription plus credits
  • Open source, no account required

Which one fits your week

Pick Opus Clip if you already create long videos and need a fast, hands-off way to slice them into clips with captions and a virality read. That's a real, valuable job and it does it well.

Pick OpenNolan if you don't have a long video to chop — you have raw clips or an idea, and you want an original vertical reel built for you, on your Mac, with full timeline control and no subscription. The two aren't mutually exclusive, but they solve different halves of the short-form problem. OpenNolan is still in development, so the way to claim early access is to join the waitlist.

Frequently asked questions

What's the real difference between OpenNolan and Opus Clip?

Starting point. Opus Clip repurposes an existing long video into short clips. OpenNolan creates an original vertical reel from raw clips, screenshots, or just an idea — no long video required. The agent sources B-roll, music, captions, and motion, then hands you a real timeline to fine-tune.

Can OpenNolan repurpose a long video like Opus Clip does?

OpenNolan is built to create original shorts rather than auto-slice long-form into many clips. You can bring in any footage you have, and the agent assembles a finished vertical cut from it. If your main need is chopping a long podcast or webinar into many clips fast, Opus Clip is purpose-built for that specific job.

Is OpenNolan available to download today?

Not yet — OpenNolan is a Mac app in active development, and this is the waitlist. Join the list and we'll email you the moment it's ready to download. Opus Clip is a live web app you can use now; the two start from different points in your workflow.

How does pricing compare — subscription vs BYOK?

Opus Clip runs on a subscription with credits, processed in the cloud. OpenNolan uses bring-your-own-key: you add your own AI provider key and pay providers directly, no markup and no account required. It runs local-first on your Mac, so your files stay on your machine, and it's open source.

Is OpenNolan Mac-only?

Yes. OpenNolan is a native macOS desktop app, not a web tool — that's part of how it keeps rendering local and your files private. Opus Clip is a web app that works across platforms in the browser. If a Mac-native, local-first workflow matters to you, that's a meaningful difference.
