See it in action

You're a founder or builder, not an editor. You want your product seen on Reels, TikTok, and Shorts — and you want the work of getting there to disappear. Submagic is a real help with one slice of that: paste in a clip and it adds fast, animated captions, suggests B-roll, drops in zooms and sound effects. If you've already cut a video, it makes it punchier.

But notice the villain hiding in that sentence: you already cut the video. The editing tax — choosing the hook, finding the clips, pacing the cuts, pulling proof, scoring it to music — still lands on you. A captioning layer decorates the result. It doesn't do the part that eats your whole week.

OpenNolan is built for the part before the captions. It's a Mac app where an AI agent assembles the entire reel from your raw clips — structure, B-roll and images from the web, screenshots with auto-highlight, music, sound effects, motion, and yes, captions too. Then you fine-tune anything on a real timeline. We're builders who'd rather ship than learn an editor, so we made the agent do the editing.

An enhancement layer vs a whole-video agent

Submagic polishes the clip. OpenNolan builds the reel the clip belongs to.

Here's the fair, structural difference. Submagic is an enhancement layer: it takes a clip you provide and makes it look like short-form — captions, emojis, zooms, B-roll suggestions, SFX. It's web-based and subscription-priced, and it's genuinely fast at that job.

OpenNolan starts a step earlier. The agent takes your raw footage (or just a description) and builds the reel from scratch — it picks the hook, places the clips, cuts to rhythm, pulls supporting B-roll and images, adds music and sound, animates overlays, and burns in captions. Submagic decorates the clip; OpenNolan does the editing.

  • Submagic: captions, zooms, emojis, B-roll suggestions on a clip you supply
  • OpenNolan: hook, structure, cuts, B-roll, screenshots, music, motion, captions
  • Submagic improves a finished cut; OpenNolan makes the cut for you

How the editing actually happens

With a captioning tool, the editing workflow is still yours. You decide the order, trim the silences, choose what's on screen — then the tool layers style on top. That's fine if you already know your way around an edit. It's a wall if you're a builder who never wanted to learn one.

OpenNolan flips it. The agent does the first pass of editing, then hands you a real NLE timeline to adjust cuts, captions, overlays, images, audio, transitions, crop, and speed. And because of our render-once, edit-cheap model, most of those tweaks — reorder, retrim, retime, reposition — preview instantly without re-rendering. You stay in control without doing the grunt work. Want the underlying moves? See our Instagram Reels playbook.

  • Agent builds the whole composition, not just the styling
  • Real timeline editor for full manual control afterward
  • Render once; most edits preview instantly, no re-render

Mac-native, local, BYOK, open source

Submagic runs in the browser on a subscription, with your files passing through its service. That's a normal, convenient model for a web tool.

OpenNolan is a different shape on purpose. It's a native macOS app that runs on your machine — your footage stays local. You bring your own AI provider key and pay providers directly, with no markup and no subscription, no account required. And it's open source, so you can see exactly how the agent works. For a deeper look at the pieces, browse the feature overview or our CapCut comparison.

  • Native Mac app, not a web subscription
  • Local-first: your files stay on your machine
  • Bring your own key — pay providers directly, no markup
  • Open source

Which one fits where you are

If you're already comfortable editing and just want faster, prettier captions on a clip you cut yourself, Submagic does that well — it's a solid enhancement layer and we won't pretend otherwise.

If the real blocker is that you don't have time to edit at all — you have raw clips and a product to ship — then a captioning tool only covers the last 10%. OpenNolan is for the founder who wants the whole reel made and then the option to tune it. The outcome we're after: scroll-stopping short-form that ships as fast as your product, without you becoming an editor.

Frequently asked questions

Is OpenNolan vs Submagic a fair comparison if Submagic is mainly a captions tool?

Yes, because the overlap is real but partial. Submagic is excellent at automatic animated captions and enhancing a clip you already edited. OpenNolan also writes burned-in captions, but it does the whole job before that — structuring the reel, cutting, pulling B-roll, adding music and motion. They meet at captions and diverge everywhere else.

Can OpenNolan make the whole video, not just style it?

That's the core idea. The AI agent assembles the entire vertical reel from your raw clips or a description: hook, fast cuts, B-roll and images from the web, screenshots with auto-highlight, music, sound effects, motion, and captions. Then you can refine any cut on a real timeline. It builds the reel rather than decorating one you already made.

Does OpenNolan use a subscription like Submagic?

No. Submagic is a subscription web tool. OpenNolan is bring-your-own-key: you add your own AI provider API key and pay those providers directly, with no markup and no subscription. There's no account to create. Your footage stays local because it's a native Mac app, and the project is open source.

Is OpenNolan available to download right now?

Not yet — OpenNolan is in active development and this is the waitlist. Submagic is live today as a web app. If a Mac-native agent that builds the whole reel (not just captions) is what you want, join the waitlist and we'll let you know the moment early access opens.

Is OpenNolan only for Mac?

Yes. OpenNolan is a native macOS desktop app, which is what lets it render locally and keep your files on your machine. Submagic, by contrast, is web-based and works across platforms in a browser. If you're on macOS and want local, open-source, bring-your-own-key short-form editing, OpenNolan is built for you.
